Initial literature reportStojsavljevic et al., 2026 · PMID 42187520

A 39-year-old woman with psoriatic arthritis, hypertension, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and anxiety developed transverse myelitis while receiving etanercept for approximately two years. Her psoriatic arthritis had been well controlled. The publication did not report the etanercept trade name, manufacturer, formulation, dose, route, frequency, lot number, or exact treatment dates. Concomitant medications included methyldopa and pantoprazole; doses and treatment dates were not reported.

Within two weeks after the last reported etanercept dose, the patient developed bilateral lower-extremity numbness, tingling, and weakness, painful hypersensitivity of the right thigh, numbness extending from the umbilicus to the right foot, and left-leg pain. She remained ambulatory but could not feel the floor. Neurologic examination showed right lower-extremity weakness rated 4/5, an umbilical sensory level, decreased pinprick and vibration sensation, brisk right patellar reflex, extensor plantar response, and a mildly right hemiparetic gait. Spinal MRI demonstrated a gadolinium-enhancing T2 hyperintense lesion at T11–L1; brain MRI was normal.

Cerebrospinal fluid contained 19 nucleated cells/µL with 97% lymphocytes, protein 49 mg/dL, glucose 54 mg/dL, four oligoclonal bands, and elevated kappa free light chains; cytology was negative. Aquaporin-4 and myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibodies were negative. An extensive infectious, metabolic, autoimmune, and paraneoplastic evaluation did not identify an alternative cause.

The patient was hospitalized and treated with intravenous methylprednisolone 1,000 mg daily for five days, with minimal improvement. Etanercept was discontinued, future TNF-inhibitor therapy was discouraged, and physical and occupational therapy were initiated. Fourteen days after discharge, she was readmitted with worsening bilateral numbness, marked right-leg weakness, and urinary retention. Repeat MRI showed expansion of the lesion from T10 to L1 with near-complete cord involvement at T11–T12, consistent with longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis.

Seven plasma-exchange treatments were administered over 14 days, followed by rehabilitation. Imaging showed lesion reduction; urinary retention resolved and strength gradually improved, although the patient had not returned to baseline at discharge. She later traveled to another country and was lost to follow-up. The authors considered etanercept-associated myelitis the leading diagnosis while noting that TNF inhibition might have unmasked or triggered multiple sclerosis or another antibody-mediated demyelinating disorder. Dechallenge was not assessable because the condition worsened after withdrawal before improving following plasma exchange. Rechallenge was not performed.

Follow-up

Open information requests

  • Confirm brand, manufacturer, formulation, dose, route, frequency, lot number, and exact treatment dates.
  • Obtain exact event-onset, hospitalization, readmission, treatment, and discharge dates.
  • Clarify final neurologic outcome, functional status, and ongoing rehabilitation.
  • Obtain repeat spinal MRI results and any repeat AQP4-IgG or MOG-IgG testing.
  • Clarify whether multiple sclerosis or another demyelinating disorder was subsequently diagnosed.
  • Perform duplicate search using patient, product, event, authors, institution, and publication details.
