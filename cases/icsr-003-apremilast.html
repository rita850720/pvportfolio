Initial literature report · Case 2Uchida et al., 2026 · PMID 42252545

A 31-year-old Japanese woman with allergic rhinitis, chronic urticaria, and recalcitrant acrodermatitis continua of Hallopeau was treated with apremilast. In February 2024, she had developed spontaneous swelling and erythema of the nail matrix involving the right middle and ring fingers, with vesicles, subungual pustules, and pain. By November 2024, pain had also developed in the distal interphalangeal joint of the left ring finger. She had no history of smoking.

Before referral, the condition remained refractory to oral antibiotics, topical corticosteroid, topical difamilast, and oral cyclosporine 100 mg/day. After referral in February 2025, radiography and joint ultrasonography showed no abnormalities, and matrix metalloproteinase-3, C-reactive protein, antinuclear antibody, and anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibody values were within normal limits. She was diagnosed with acrodermatitis continua and began oral apremilast 30 mg twice daily.

After two months, the erythema and swelling improved significantly, while the distal interphalangeal joint pain persisted. During apremilast treatment, the patient developed headaches. The publication did not report the precise headache-onset date, severity, frequency, treatment, or associated findings. In June 2025, treatment was switched to risankizumab 150 mg at weeks 0 and 4 and then every 12 weeks. Joint pain subsequently improved; the outcome of the headache was not reported.

The headache was assessed as non-serious and expected. A reasonable causal possibility was retained because it developed during treatment and contributed to the therapy change; however, alternative causes were not discussed. Dechallenge was not assessable because the post-switch headache outcome was not reported. Rechallenge was not performed.

Follow-up

Open information requests

  • Confirm the exact apremilast start and stop dates, titration, trade name, and lot number.
  • Obtain headache onset date, frequency, severity, duration, associated symptoms, and treatment.
  • Clarify whether headache resolved after the switch to risankizumab and the time to recovery.
  • Obtain concomitant medications used during treatment and relevant alternative etiologies.
  • Confirm the treating clinician’s event-causality assessment.
  • Perform duplicate search and distinguish the newly reported patient from historical cases summarized in the review.

QC checkpoints

Source-to-case reconciliation

  • Off-label ACH use captured separately from the adverse event.
  • Pre-existing joint pain not coded as a treatment-emergent event.
  • Skin and joint improvement not used as the headache outcome.
  • Earlier cases in the literature review treated as secondary sources and potential duplicates.
