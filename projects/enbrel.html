Enbrel and the U.S. etanercept product family

A reference-product and biosimilar family review integrating current labeling, FDA class and pediatric surveillance, and a multi-field openFDA FAERS/AEMS cohort.

Surveillance question

How can a broad U.S. etanercept family cohort support label-based surveillance while avoiding unsupported comparisons among the reference product and biosimilars?

Product-family context

The family includes the reference product Enbrel and Enbrel Mini (etanercept), Erelzi and Erelzi Sensoready (etanercept-szzs), and Eticovo (etanercept-ykro). The current Enbrel label carries a boxed warning for serious infections and malignancies. Additional labeled risks include neurologic events, heart failure, hematologic reactions, hepatitis B reactivation, allergic reactions, and autoimmunity.

Multi-name search

The search retained all FDA-verified product, presentation, generic, proper-name, and biosimilar aliases, including zero-result Eticovo and etanercept-ykro terms. Enbrel/reference and generic etanercept terms dominated the data; Erelzi terms contributed a much smaller overlapping subset.

Interpretation

Injection-site PTs showed strong descriptive concordance with the label. Infection concepts aligned with common-infection labeling and the boxed serious-infection warning, but counts cannot distinguish minor from serious product-attributable infections. Rheumatoid arthritis, arthralgia, joint swelling, pain, and psoriasis may substantially reflect approved indications or underlying disease. The broad family cohort is suitable for surveillance priority setting, not comparison among Enbrel, Erelzi, and Eticovo.
