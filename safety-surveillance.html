Reading the results

What these case studies can and cannot show

OpenFDA supports transparent descriptive surveillance, but report counts require strong interpretive boundaries.

Can support

Search reproducibility, descriptive PT distributions, outcome-flag summaries, temporal patterns, and focused case-review priorities.

Cannot establish

Incidence, prevalence, comparative safety, product-attributable risk, or causality.

Safety-specific reproducibility

Safety surveillance methodology

This workflow is distinct from the publication-based method used in Literature Surveillance. Its unit of analysis is the openFDA drug adverse-event report record, interpreted within FDA labeling and regulatory context.

End-to-end workflow

Define product and authorization scope
Verify names, labels, and application history
Check FDA communications and signal postings
Construct a multi-field openFDA query
Summarize PTs and outcome flags
Screen prespecified AESIs
Compare patterns with labeling
Document limits and next steps

Product-family definition

Each review begins with FDA-verified proprietary names, proper or generic names, active-substance names, presentations, application numbers, and—when relevant—biosimilars. Search terms are tested across reported medicinal-product, reported active-substance, and normalized openFDA brand and generic fields.

Why this matters: Drug names are not fully normalized in openFDA. Field-level matches overlap, and generic records may not identify a specific proprietary product.

Regulatory context

The current U.S. Prescribing Information anchors the safety profile. Drugs@FDA records, FDA safety communications, AEMS potential-signal postings, pediatric reviews, the Purple Book, and other FDA pages are checked where relevant. Label-bearing submissions are not automatically counted as new safety signals.

Descriptive analysis

  • Cohort: any record satisfying the prespecified name-and-field query and date restriction.
  • Top PTs: exact MedDRA Preferred Term count endpoints; one record can contain several PTs.
  • Seriousness and outcomes: FDA report flags; death, hospitalization, life-threatening, disability, congenital anomaly, and other-serious flags can overlap.
  • Time trends: received-date counts interpreted as reporting volume, not risk trends.
  • AESIs: report-level screens using prespecified exact PT groupings derived from the current label or a clearly identified FDA surveillance question.

Interpretive guardrails

A spontaneous report does not establish that a product caused an event. There is no exposed-patient denominator, and reporting is influenced by market duration, publicity, prescribing volume, disease context, reporting practices, missing data, duplicate versions, and product-name quality. Custom exact-PT AESIs are screening constructs, not validated SMQs or clinically adjudicated cases.

Reproducibility retained in each case study

The portfolio records product aliases, searched fields, date boundaries, dataset update and cutoff, exact cohort specification, report totals, PT and outcome definitions, AESI groupings, regulatory sources, and interpretation limits.
